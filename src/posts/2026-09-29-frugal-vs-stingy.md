**Frugal** emphasizes using money or resources carefully. **Stingy** criticizes someone for being unwilling to spend or give, especially when generosity or a reasonable expense is expected.

The amount spent can be identical. The difference is often what the speaker thinks about the choice.

## The same lunch, two different stories

“Maya brings leftovers to work so she can save for a trip.” You might call her **frugal**: she is avoiding waste and choosing where her money goes.

“Maya invites a friend to lunch, then refuses to contribute to the shared meal.” **Stingy** fits the criticism: the issue is a reluctance to give a reasonable share.

Frugal is often approving or neutral. Stingy is disapproving. Calling someone stingy can turn a description of their spending into a judgment about their generosity.

## What about thrifty and parsimonious?

**Thrifty** is close to frugal and often suggests resourcefulness: repairing a coat, comparing prices, or putting leftovers to good use.

**Parsimonious** depends on context. Describing a person as parsimonious can sound critical of extreme economy. In a phrase such as “a parsimonious explanation,” it instead means economical in its assumptions. A single broad synonym like “sparing” would miss that distinction.

## Practice the contrast, not just the definitions

Write two sentences about someone spending very little. In one, make the choice sensible. In the other, make it ungenerous. Then choose a word for each sentence.

Return to the pair later and try recalling the words without looking. The useful question is not simply “Do these words relate to money?” It is “What judgment does this sentence call for?”
